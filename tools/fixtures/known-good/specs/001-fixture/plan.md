## The shapes that must not fire

Each of these pairs a count with a range — they are inside the rule's
population, not outside it — and each would be reported wrongly by a pattern
that read it naively. Every one is a construction the real corpus uses, and
each carries a count that *disagrees* with the naive reading on purpose, so an
arm that stops working goes red instead of going quiet.

A **superseded count**, struck and left visible rather than deleted, beside a
range that is still live. The corpus supersedes this way everywhere, so reading
struck text as a claim would make the convention unsatisfiable. The strike ends
before the bounds do on purpose: with the range struck too there would be fewer
than two live identifiers and the pairing would fail for that reason instead,
which would leave this arm proving nothing. ~~**two** owner decisions~~ **four**
were recorded (OD-01 through OD-04).

A **struck intermediate bound**, which is how a range is advanced without
deleting what it replaced. A pattern that takes the first identifier after the
connector reads the superseded **OD-02** as the live bound and reports a
disagreement that does not exist: **four** owner decisions (OD-01 through
~~**OD-02**~~ ~~**OD-03**~~ **OD-04**) are recorded here.

A **subset claim**, which is the commonest range in this corpus and is not a
statement of the register's size at all: **three** owner decisions (OD-03
through OD-04 and one still unrecorded) arrived late in the programme.

A **list rather than a range**. Two identifiers side by side with no connector
between them name two entries, not the span from one to the other: **two**
owner decisions (OD-01, OD-04) were taken by the same author.
